# Intelligence Tutorial · Tutor Guide

Oct 15, 2026 · @Kit Double

Is it an intelligence? is a 60-minute team tutorial on Lectures 4 to 9. Students do a 12-minute battery on their laptops or phones, the class's data appear live on the projector, then teams put a candidate intelligence on trial against the six criteria. Two points: the positive manifold is real, and **the measure shapes the conclusion**.

## Setup

Open **usyd-meta-lab.github.io/intelligence-tutorial** on the room computer and click **Start a session**.

- Each tutorial gets its own session code. Never use "Rehearse offline" in class.
- Set the number of teams in the lobby (default 6, about 5 students each). Candidates go in team order: A Trait EI, B Ability EI, C Creativity, D Practical intelligence, E Social cognition (theory of mind), F Musical intelligence, G "AI-prompting intelligence", H Metacognitive ability.
- Students scan the QR code, pick a team and stay anonymous, or open the Canvas link and enter the session code.
- Students move through the battery at their own pace, then wait. The "Finished" counter shows when to press →. Charts update live.

| Key on the projector | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| → / ← | Next / previous phase |
| P | Hide tutor controls and prompts |
| 1–9 | Switch reveal tabs |

## Run sheet

| Phase | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Lobby, then Hook: EQ vs IQ | Students join teams, then vote on "Emotional intelligence matters more than IQ for success in life." |
| The battery | Six short tasks. Move on when about 80% have finished. |
| Reveal: g & EI | Tabs 1a to 1d |
| Reveal: creativity | Tabs 2a to 2c |
| Scorecard | Teams score their candidate on the six criteria and design one study. Start the 10-minute timer on screen. |
| Cross-examination | 60 seconds per team; the next team asks one question. Then the lectures' verdicts tab. |
| Vote again, then Wrap-up | Same poll, the before/after chart, then the close. |

**Short of time?** Skip tabs 1b and 2c, and give cross-examination to three teams only.

## The battery

| Part | Task | Measures | Time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Ten self-report items, 1 to 7 | Trait EI (6), emotional stability (3), "I am creative" (1) | about 1.5 min |
| 2 | Word meanings | Gc | 75 s |
| 3 | Number series | Gf | 2 min |
| 4 | Same or mirror? (rotated letters) | Gv, scored right minus wrong | 1 min |
| 5 | Ten emotion-understanding questions, then "how many did you get right?" | Ability EI, plus a metacognitive prediction | untimed, about 3 min |
| 6 | Uses for a brick | Divergent thinking: fluency and originality | 2 min |

Self-report comes first, so students rate their emotional skills before they see the emotion questions. Timed tasks have a "Start the clock" button, so nobody loses time reading instructions.

**Framing matters.** Say out loud: "This is a demo of how psychologists measure abilities, not a test of you." It is the stereotype-threat point from Lecture 6, and you can come back to it later: why did I say that at the start?

## Reveals

### Reveal 1a: Do the thinking tasks hang together?

&#91;image: Reveal 1a: correlations between the three thinking tasks and their loadings on g\]

*Simulated class, for illustration. The projector shows your class live.*

**Expect:** Positive correlations between vocabulary, number series and rotation, with one factor capturing a large share of the variance. The third key stat is the noise band: with about 30 students, any correlation under about ±.36 could be chance.

**Ask:** Why should knowing what "laconic" means predict how fast you can rotate a letter? g is a statistical extraction, what is common to diverse tasks, not a thing in the head. If one correlation comes out near zero, it is a sample-size lesson, not a refutation: Carroll reanalysed more than 460 data sets.

### Reveal 1b: Everything we measured

&#91;image: Reveal 1b: correlation matrix of all nine measures\]

*Simulated class, for illustration.*

**Expect:** Three clusters: the thinking tasks; trait EI with emotional stability; brick fluency with originality.

**Ask:** Which measure sits where you'd expect an "intelligence" to sit? Every cell is a correlation between *measures*, each a narrow window on its construct.

### Reveal 1c: Two kinds of "emotional intelligence"

&#91;image: Reveal 1c: ability EI and trait EI correlated with g and with emotional stability\]

*Simulated class, for illustration.*

**Expect:** Ability EI (the emotion questions) correlates modestly with g and little with emotional stability. Trait EI (self-report) shows the reverse. The two EIs barely agree.

**Ask:** If you only had the trait measure, what would you conclude about EI? And with only the ability measure? This is Lecture 7's "the measure determines the conclusion", in your own class.

### Reveal 1d: Do you know how good you are with emotions?

&#91;image: Reveal 1d: predicted vs actual emotion score, and self-rated EI vs actual score\]

*Simulated class, for illustration.*

**Expect:** Predicted scores relate only weakly to actual scores, and self-rated EI barely predicts the emotion questions.

**Ask:** Self-reported ability is not actual ability. Predicting your own score is a metacognitive judgement (Lectures 1 to 3). Trait EI may still measure something useful, such as emotional self-efficacy, just not ability.

### Reveal 2a: More ideas = more original?

&#91;image: Reveal 2a: fluency against total and average originality\]

*Simulated class, for illustration.*

**Expect:** Total originality correlates very strongly with fluency; average originality per idea much less.

**Ask:** Each idea scores 1 minus the share of the class who had a similar idea. Same answers, a different scoring rule, a different "most creative" person. Which rule would you defend? (The fluency confound, Lecture 9.)

### Reveal 2b: What the room said

&#91;image: Reveal 2b: share of the class giving each category of brick use\]

*Simulated class, for illustration.*

**Expect:** Doorstop, building, paperweight and weapon dominate. Pale bars (under 15% of the class) count as rare.

**Ask:** Rarity depends on who is in the room, and the categories come from a keyword list somebody wrote. Is "throw it through a window" creative? Creativity needs novelty *and* usefulness. Who should judge? (Amabile's consensual assessment.)

**Before projecting one-off answers:** click **Show some one-off answers** and check them on your own screen first. They are free text from students.

### Reveal 2c: Is creativity separate from g?

&#91;image: Reveal 2c: g composite against total originality\]

*Simulated class, for illustration.*

**Expect:** At most a small positive correlation between g and originality.

**Ask:** Creativity is partly dependent on g but not reducible to it; openness is its strongest trait correlate. The threshold hypothesis can't be tested here: what sample would you need?

## Scorecard and cross-examination

Each team's phone shows its candidate, a short brief and where to look for evidence. As a team they mark each criterion **Meets / Partly / Fails / No evidence**, write the one study that would convince a sceptic, and give an overall verdict. Anyone in the team can tap, and it syncs to all their phones.

While they work, push on two things: "No evidence" is not the same as "Fails", and incremental validity means beyond g **and** personality. Teams with trait EI, ability EI and creativity should cite today's reveals.

In cross-examination the projector shows every team's scorecard and study. Team B questions Team A, Team C questions Team B, and so on. Good questions:

- Is that a factor separate from g, or g applied to new content?
- Does it predict beyond g and personality?
- Would your study tell an intelligence apart from expertise?

Finish on **The lectures' verdicts** tab. Disagreeing with a lecture, with a good argument, is a better outcome than agreeing from memory.

## Before they leave

Show the before/after poll on the wrap-up screen and ask one student who changed their vote what changed their mind. Close with: **"Smart at what, measured how, and how do we know?"** When you meet any claim of a new intelligence, run the six criteria.

## If something goes wrong

- **A phone or laptop is stuck on an old screen:** it re-checks every few seconds; otherwise reload the page (answers are kept).
- **Some students are still on the battery:** move on anyway. The reveals use whoever has finished, and late students can keep going.
- **Fewer than about 15 finished students, or a messy pattern:** press **Load simulated class**. It adds 25 simulated students on the projector only, never saved and never shown on phones. It is a fixed class that shows the textbook pattern every time, so it is also good for rehearsing.
- **Red "Can't reach the live database" banner:** try another network, or tell Kit.
- **Don't press Clear session data during class:** it deletes the session's responses.

## Things to say

- Start: "This is a demo of how psychologists measure abilities, not a test of you."
- Answers are anonymous. During the reveals each phone shows a private summary of that student's own results, with a reminder that a minute per task says nothing meaningful about them.
- Lecture links: L4 positive manifold, g, CHC, Gardner; L6 stereotype threat; L7 ability vs trait EI; L8 the six criteria and practical intelligence; L9 divergent thinking and the fluency confound.
- Close: "The measure shapes the conclusion."
