Key Concepts

01
 Lecture 4

The positive manifold

Scores on very different cognitive tasks (vocabulary, number series, mental rotation) correlate positively. Spearman's g is the general factor extracted from those correlations. It is a statistical summary of what the tasks share, not a single brain mechanism, and not the whole of intelligence.

02
 Lecture 4

Sample size and noise

With about 30 people, a correlation needs to be roughly ±.36 before we can be confident it isn't chance. Small samples make correlations wobble, which is why the structure of intelligence rests on hundreds of data sets (Carroll, 1993), not one class.

03
 Lecture 7

Ability EI vs trait EI

Ability EI is measured with questions that have right and wrong answers (e.g. the MSCEIT). It correlates modestly with g. Trait EI is self-reported (e.g. the TEIQue) and overlaps heavily with personality, especially emotional stability. They are different constructs, so whether "EI" looks like a new intelligence depends on the measure.

04
 Lectures 1–3, 7

Self-report is not ability

How good you say you are with emotions predicts only weakly how well you do on emotion questions. Predicting your own score is a metacognitive judgement, and it can be poorly calibrated, just like confidence in the metacognition module.

05
 Lecture 9

Divergent thinking

The Alternative Uses Task scores fluency (how many ideas), flexibility (how many kinds) and originality (how rare). Originality scored by statistical rarity depends on the sample and on who decides the categories, and creativity also needs usefulness, not just novelty.

06
 Lecture 9

The fluency confound

Total originality rises simply because you produced more ideas. Scoring the average originality per idea removes much of that link. Same answers, different scoring rule, a different person comes out "most creative": the measure shapes the conclusion.

07
Scorecard · Lectures 7–9

When is it an intelligence?

Six criteria: does it form a factor separable from g? Can it be measured with right and wrong answers? Does it predict beyond g and personality? Does it have a developmental trajectory? Is it neurally dissociable? Does it behave like a stable trait? The scorecard works for any new construct claim.

Discussion question

Does emotional intelligence matter more than IQ for success in life?

You'll vote at the start and again at the end. Before you change your mind (or don't), ask which EI the claim is about, how it was measured, and whether it predicts success beyond g and personality. The question that runs through the whole module: smart at what, measured how, and how do we know?
