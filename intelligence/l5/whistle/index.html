Play a rising tone. Students raise a hand, then lower it when they can no longer hear anything. The readout shows where the room drops out.

Turn your computer volume down before you press play, then bring it up until the first tone is comfortable. High tones can be piercing.

Presenter notes
  • Laptop speakers often roll off above about 15 to 16 kHz, so a room can look like it is dropping out early because of the speaker. If you can, use external speakers and test the top of the range yourself first.
  • Hearing range at the top end shrinks with age and with noise exposure. Younger students tend to hear to higher frequencies than older staff, so a staff member in the room is a good comparison.
  • Tones near the top of the range may be inaudible to everyone. If the whole room drops out at once, the speaker or the sample rate is the likely cause.
  • Use the single-tone buttons to settle disagreements: press a frequency and count hands.
