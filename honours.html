Project themes, a roadmap for first semester and the proposal, for students doing Honours in the Meta Lab.

Project themes

Each year we choose three areas of interest that come with ready-to-use paradigms. This year all three use the same one, the advice giver paradigm, which you can watch in action below the project list. Choose from the three below, and tell Kit your preferences in the first week or two of Honours.

  • 01

    Does helping a lot make people think their advice is more helpful?

    People often judge how well something works partly by how much they have done it. This project asks whether advice givers who give advice more often come to believe that their advice is more helpful, regardless of whether it actually is. Using the advice giver paradigm, students will examine how the frequency of helping shapes judgements of efficacy.

  • 02

    Do people miss the benefit of their help when the improvement is delayed?

    Help often does not produce immediate change, and the benefit may appear only later. This project asks whether advice givers fail to credit themselves for improvement that arrives after a delay between the advice and the recipient’s improvement.

  • 03

    Do people higher in emotional intelligence help differently?

    This project asks whether people higher in emotional intelligence (EI) choose different strategies when giving advice, and whether they vary those strategies more in response to feedback about whether the advice worked.

The advice giver paradigm

Participants are matched with a partner in another room. One person views negative images, and the other, the advice giver, sends advice on some trials, sees how their partner says they feel, and later rates how effective their advice was. The animation steps through a session from the cover story to the debrief.

First semester roadmap

A rough guide to how it usually unfolds, from your first meeting to your second experiment. Each step is roughly one meeting, but the pace will vary from project to project. Pick a step to see what to prepare beforehand and what we aim to settle when we meet.

Proposal

Write a short proposal of about 1,000 words. The background explains why the method matters. The method is the detailed, useful part, and it is what gets the feedback. Aim to make the method easy to judge and improve.

Background

  • Keep it to about 500 words.
  • Explain the problem, and why it’s important.
  • It is basically the opening and the research questions sections of the introduction in the writing guide.
  • Give enough context for good feedback. You don’t need a full literature review.
  • AI can help it read smoothly, but treat that as polishing. The proposal is not marked.

Method

  • Keep it to about 500 words.
  • Make it detailed and concrete, so readers can tell whether the study will actually work.
  • Use figures where they help explain the procedure, the sequence of a trial or the materials.
  • AI can help draft and tighten it, but aim for clarity and feasibility rather than polish.
