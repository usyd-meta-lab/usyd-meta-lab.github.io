# Metacognition Tutorial · Tutor Guide

Oct 15, 2026 · @Kit Double

Do you know what you know? is a 60-minute team pub quiz on confidence. Students play on their laptops of phones; the class's data appear live on the projector after each round.

## Setup

Open **usyd-meta-lab.github.io/metacognition-tutorial** on the room computer and click **Start a session**.

- Each tutorial gets its own session code. Never use "Rehearse offline" in class.
- Students scan the QR code, pick a team (4–5 per team) and stay anonymous or go to the link (it's on Canvas) and enter the session code that you generate.
- Students move through each round at their own pace, then wait. The "Finished" counter shows when to press →. Charts update live.

| Key on the projector | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| → / ← | Next / previous phase |
| P | Hide tutor controls and prompts |
| 1–9 | Switch reveal tabs |

## Run sheet

Each reveal follows its round, so you can stop at any point.

| Phase | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Lobby | QR code, students join teams |
| R1 Tip of the tongue | Recall + feeling of knowing |
| R2 How sure? | 16 items, each with a confidence rating |
| Quick question, then Reveal: confidence | Tabs 2a and 2b |
| R3 Pass or play | +1 right, −2 wrong, 0 for a pass |
| Reveal: recall & passing | Tabs 1 and 3 |
| R4 Team round | Answer alone, then agree a team answer |
| Reveal: teams + leaderboard | Tab 4, then the leaderboard |
| Exam-prep reflection | Discussion question |

## Reveals

### After R2 · Reveal 2a: When you said 80%, were you right 80% of the time?

&#91;embedded content: Simulated data, for illustration · the projector shows your class live\]

**Expect:** Black = all items, grey = normal, orange = deceptive. Points below the dashed line show overconfidence, worst on the deceptive items. Calibration = mean confidence − mean accuracy (Lecture 1).

**Ask:** Which question fooled the room? Now that you know, will you play differently in round 3?

### After R2 · Reveal 2b: Calibration vs resolution

&#91;embedded content: Simulated data, for illustration · the projector shows your class live\]

**Expect:** Each dot is one student. Left–right = calibration in points; up–down = resolution, the gamma correlation between confidence and being right (−1 to +1). The two are separate: look for a dot near zero calibration but low gamma.

**Ask:** Can you be perfectly calibrated and still not know which answers were right? Link to Lecture 1: calibration is a bias question, resolution a sensitivity question. Gamma is undefined for anyone with no errors, one reason the field moved to meta-d′.

### After R3 · Reveal 1: You couldn't recall it, but did you know you'd recognise it?

&#91;embedded content: Simulated data, for illustration · the projector shows your class live\]

**Expect:** Bars rise from left to right: people knew something about answers they could not retrieve.

**Ask:** What were you monitoring, if not the answer itself? (Monitoring without retrieval; Hart, 1965.)

### After R3 · Reveal 3: Did passing pay?

&#91;embedded content: Simulated data, for illustration · the projector shows your class live\]

**Expect:** Played answers were far more accurate than passed ones. Most students beat "play everything".

**Ask:** Monitoring is only worth points when it drives control. Did seeing reveal 2 change how you played?

### After R4 · Reveal 4: Did your team beat its members?

&#91;embedded content: Simulated data, for illustration · the projector shows your class live\]

**Expect:** Grey = average member alone, orange = the team's agreed answer, black = best member alone. Teams beat their average member, rarely their best.

**Ask:** How did your team decide? Did the most confident voice win? On deceptive items, why can't discussion help? (Bahrami et al., 2010.)

### Leaderboard

Total = average member score (R1 recall + R2 correct + R3 pass-or-play points) + team round × 2 (R4 questions the team got right, doubled). Also shows the best-calibrated team.

## Before they leave

Discussion question: **What would you change when studying for the exam based on this class?** Each student writes down one change, based on today's data.

**Short of time?** Jump to Reveal: teams for the leaderboard, or to Wrap-up.

## If something goes wrong

- **A phone****/****l****aptop**** is stuck on an old screen:** it re-checks every few seconds; otherwise reload the page (answers are kept).
- **Discussion runs long:** No worries. Each reveal follows its round, so stop wherever you are.
- **Fewer than about 12 students:** press **Load simulated class**. It exists on the projector only and is never saved.
- **Red "Can't reach the live database" banner:** try another network, or tell Kit.
- **Don't press Clear session data during class:** it deletes the session's responses.

## Things to say

- Start: "Points for being right, and for knowing *when* you're right."
- Answers are anonymous. Each phone shows a private summary of that student's own results during the reveals.
- Lecture links: L1 monitoring, control, calibration vs resolution; L2 Dunning–Kruger; L3 study regulation and groups.
- Close: "Confidence is only useful when it tracks accuracy."
